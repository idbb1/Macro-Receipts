## Seat 1 — The Positioning Seat
### *Seth Godin methodology — positioning, trust, remarkability*

**Verdict on the brief in one sentence**
The dataset is a real moat and the six principles are honest work, but the stated goal — "post it on X for engagement so influencers can be scored" — has the causality backwards, and left unchanged it builds a well-sourced pillory that thirty people will spend a decade discrediting.

**The reframe** — four things, in order of how much they cost you.

**1. Engagement is a symptom, not a goal.** Nobody has ever built a durable thing by aiming at the symptom. Before a single tactic: *Who's it for?* — the person who spends six hours a week on macro podcasts, has an allocation decision they keep deferring, and has quietly stopped believing the confident voice in their headphones but has no evidence to justify the feeling. *What's it for?* — to convert "I think that guy is full of it" into "here's the date, here's the quote, here's what happened," so they can allocate attention with the discipline they already apply to capital. Smallest viable audience: **1,000 of those people who would genuinely miss this if it vanished.** Not "investors." Not "fintwit." A thousand named-in-your-head people.

**2. You are scoring the wrong noun.** "Most accurate" is a metric partly made of luck, and everybody knows it, which is why it will be attacked forever. It also manufactures one friend and twenty-nine enemies. **Score accountability.** Did you make a dated, falsifiable, specific claim? Is it still up? Did you say what happened when it resolved? Accuracy is contaminated; accountability is 100% a choice, entirely attributable, and something a person is *proud* to display at a 48% hit rate. That flips the whole tribe: thirty potential allies instead of one. The enemy stops being "wrong forecasters" — a group of named humans — and becomes a behaviour: **claims made without a date.**

**3. The repo already knows the name. The brief forgot it.** The folder says Macro-Receipts; the brief says "Macro Scoreboard." A scoreboard implies a loser. A receipt implies a record — and "receipts" is *already the audience's own word*, which means the remark is pre-installed. Kill "scoreboard" everywhere. Three nouns, one job each: a **receipt** (the unit), **The Record** (the table), **The Ledger** (the weekly briefing).

**4. What travels is one receipt, not the table.** Apply the logo test: strip the branding from your weekly ranked-table image and it means nothing — a list of names with numbers is unreadable without the method attached. Strip the logo from a single receipt — quote, date, source, outcome — and it is *still completely legible and still devastating*. Build the atomic unit first. The table is a byproduct.

The remark, in their words, when they forward it: **"Someone's actually keeping the receipts."** That is affiliation status — people like us write things down. The dominance version, in the DM: "He said it in March. Here's the date."

### 1. Engagement — ranked

**1. Ship the receipt as a permalinked object.** Every graded call gets its own URL — `macro-receipts.com/r/2025-03-11-gold-4000` — and its own image. That is the shareable primitive. Fixed card:

```
RECEIPT #0412                        MACRO-RECEIPTS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────
[Name] · [outlet] · 11 March 2025

"[exact verbatim quote, one or two lines]"

CALL      Gold ≥ $4,000/oz
DEADLINE  31 Dec 2025
RESULT    [ HIT ]   $4,118 · LBMA PM · 31 Dec 2025
ON RECORD 295 days
───────────────────────────────────────────────────
source: [url]          rules: /rules
```

Detail that will bite you: **never mock this up with an invented quote, not even internally.** Fabricated screenshots leak, and one fake quote attributed to a real person ends the project. Use `[QUOTE PENDING]` in every draft.

**2. Tension, not scores: the open-call clock.** The brief treats resolving calls as a notification feature. It is the entire return-visit mechanic. A resolved call is history; an *open* call is a story the reader wants finished. Permanent module, top of every page and the last line of every weekly post: **"On the clock: 11 calls resolve before 31 October."** Nobody comes back for a ranking. They come back to find out if the guy was right.

**3. Permission, defined literally.** Followers are rented. The one ask: on every forecaster page, a single line — *"Follow [name]'s calls. We'll email you twice: when they make a new dated call, and when one resolves. Nothing else."* Anticipated, personal, relevant, by construction. Target 5,000 of these before you optimise anything else. That list is the only asset here that a platform cannot take away.

**4. "Send us a receipt."** Readers submit a claim they remember hearing; you find it, timestamp it, grade it, and credit them by handle on the receipt. Turns an audience into contributors, and it is the cheapest possible sourcing. Cap it, queue it, human-review it.

**5. Cadence as a promise, not a schedule.** Same slot, 100 weeks running, no exceptions. The reliability *is* the product demo: we are the people who keep records. Missing a week doesn't cost you a week, it costs you the claim.

### 2. Revenue — ranked

1. **Nothing for six months except building the list.** Revenue that costs you credibility costs you the moat, and the moat is the only thing here that compounds.
2. **One sponsor, category-restricted, in The Ledger.** Not before 5,000 verified emails. $2,000/month for a single presenting slot. Restriction, published: **no sponsor may be a person or firm we score, sell forecasts, or sell a view.** Tools and infrastructure only. Write the restriction on the sponsor page; it is worth more as public copy than the marginal dollar it costs you.
3. **Make the refusal the marketing.** One line, on the footer of every page and the pinned reply of every weekly post: **"We take no money from anyone we score."** That sentence is a moat. It is also the sentence that lets a #1-ranked forecaster retweet you without being accused of buying the position.
4. **The honest paid rung, from month 12: "Your Own Record."** $9/month. You log your own dated calls; the same mechanical rules grade you; you get a private receipt history and a shareable badge. It sells the *method*, not the audience, and every subscriber becomes a walking demo.
5. **X revenue share: bank it, never design for it.** The instant a payout influences what you post, it turns you into the thing you're auditing.

### 3. The share — ranked

1. **The promise sentence, used everywhere unchanged:** **"We write down what they said. Then we write down what happened."** Bio, header, first line of the methodology page. No adjectives, no "AI-powered," no "definitive."
2. **Publish the misses of the person at the top.** Every profile shows *Three calls they got wrong* directly under the headline number, with equal typographic weight. Four jobs: makes the #1 more credible when they share it, removes the accusation of flattery, means you never need the bottom of the table for drama, and makes the ranking survive contact with a hostile reader.
3. **Give away the whole dataset.** Public CSV of every graded call, updated weekly, CC-BY with attribution. The rules page in full, including cases where your own rules produced a result you disliked. It makes every quant on X an unpaid auditor, and the first person who finds an error and gets a public correction becomes your loudest advocate.
4. **A "Corrections" page with real entries on it.** Dated, permanent, linked from the footer. Zero entries reads as marketing. Four entries reads as true.
5. **What never ships:** the bottom five, "worst forecaster," "exposed," "destroyed," "aged like milk," or any quote-tweet of a person's bad call in a mocking frame.

### 4. Look, feel, style, format

**It is a document, not a dashboard.** Every macro product on earth looks like a Bloomberg terminal with a dark theme; that is a crowded aesthetic and it signals "another guy with opinions."

- **Paper, not screen.** Ground `#F7F5F0`, ink `#14140F`. The *only* colour on the entire product is the result stamp: `HIT` in a stamped green-black, `MISS` in `#B3261E`, `VOID` in grey, set in a boxed monospace label at a slight rotation, like an ink stamp. When the only colour in the system is the verdict, the verdict is unmissable at thumbnail size.
- **Type: two faces, one job each.** The quote is the hero — set large in a text serif (Source Serif or similar). All data — dates, tickers, thresholds, prices — in a mono (IBM Plex Mono). Rules and hairlines instead of cards and shadows. No gradients, no glass, no charts on the share cards.
- **Format on X:** 4:5 image, one receipt, legible at thumbnail with the phone at arm's length — quote at most 18 words, verdict stamp readable at 120px wide. Text in the post, link in the first reply.
- **Web homepage is one receipt, not a table.** The single most interesting resolution of the week, full-bleed, "See the full record" below the fold. A sortable table teaches the visitor this is a database. A receipt teaches them it's a case.
- **No logo on the card except a small wordmark top-right.** If the design needs the logo to make sense, the design isn't finished.

### My one non-negotiable

**No public artefact ever contains a negative superlative about a named human.** No worst, no bottom five, no ranking counted from the bottom, no dunk in a reply, no exception for someone who "deserves it." The moment you post one, you get your biggest week ever and permanently lose the thing that makes this defensible: that anyone you score could read it and concede it's fair. Publish every miss; never publish a verdict on a person. The difference between a scoreboard and a pillory is not in the data — it's in the copy, and it's decided by whoever writes the caption at 7am on a Tuesday. Write the rule down before that person is tired.

### What I would cut

- **Affiliate links to the forecasters you grade.** Structural conflict, and the first thing a hostile quote-tweet will use.
- **The roster: 30 → 12.** Thirty names is a directory; twelve is a canon. A canon is a position; a directory is a utility.
- **"Later daily."** Being mediocre at daily loses to being the best in the world at one weekly artefact. Quit daily before you start it.
- **Rank numbers below the tier line.** Keep everyone visible with their full record — "same rigour for everyone" requires it — but list them alphabetically, not as positions 13 through 30. No ladder to the bottom.
- **Sortable-table-as-homepage.**

### Where I expect to disagree with the other seats

**Vaynerchuk.** He'll diagnose "you're not posting enough," and on receipts he's right — forty a week if you have them. But I'll fight volume of *takes*. Every extra opinion this account has makes every receipt worth less, because our whole standing rests on having no view. From the brand account, a reply is a receipt or it doesn't get sent. **Sutherland.** We agree on costly signalling. We'll fight over mockery-as-distribution: being mocked for the *format* is free reach; being mocked for humiliating a named person recruits a permanent opposition. **Musk.** I'll take his speed and most of his deletions, but hold a floor at twelve forecasters — below about ten you can't honestly say "consensus," and the product reads as a vendetta against four people. "Fury is distribution" is precisely the trade this seat exists to refuse: fury has a half-life of a week; a citation from a scored forecaster has a half-life of years. **Brunson.** Hard fight on affiliate revenue and on selling to cold traffic before the record exists. But I concede his best point immediately, because the brief has the flaw he'll spot: **there is no human name on this thing.** A faceless data brand can't be trusted with the job of judging people. Put one editor's name and face on the methodology page, the corrections page, and every weekly post.

## Seat 2 — The Distribution Seat
### *Gary Vaynerchuk methodology — X-native attention, volume, community*

**Verdict on the brief in one sentence**
The product thinking is unusually good and the distribution thinking is a single weekly post, which means you have built a Ferrari and scheduled it to drive once every seven days.

**The reframe**
The brief says the goal is to "post it on X for engagement so influencers can be scored." Invert it. **The forecasters are not the subject of the content. They are the distribution channel.** Thirty macro voices with a combined audience in the millions is the best structural setup in the brief, and §3 spends it on one tag-and-hope post a week. Every asset should be engineered around one question: *would the person named in it screenshot this into their own feed without being asked?* If yes, you are borrowing an audience you did not pay for. If no, it is a post.

Second reframe, blunter: **you are not posting enough, and the brief bakes that in.** "Twelve-plus months of clean data before UI" is correct for the moat and catastrophic for the account. Fifty-two at-bats a year cannot find a winning format. You need 1,300 at-bats a year to learn what actually spreads, and you need them starting Friday — with eight forecasters and ninety backfilled calls, not thirty-one and twelve months. The dataset compounds in the background. The account compounds in public. Start both this week or the dataset arrives to an empty room.

Third: the grading process is better content than the grade. Nobody has seen a call get resolved in public before. Document it. It is free.

### 1. Engagement — ranked

1. **Make the resolution the event, not the table.** A ranked table is a state; states are boring. A call resolving is an event, and events have a clock. Every open call with a deadline is a scheduled drama you own.
2. **Ship the "Resolving This Week" post every Friday.** The single highest-value habit unit in the whole product, and the brief doesn't have it. Four to six open calls with deadlines inside seven days, each with the quote and what price it needs. Stickiness is not a feature; it is an unresolved question with a date on it.
3. **Live-post the grading.** Screen recording, 40 seconds, no edit: pulling the price series, applying the published rule, stamping hit or miss. This converts the methodology page — which nobody reads — into the most trust-building content you will make.
4. **Reply is a channel, not admin.** Twenty replies a day under tracked forecasters' posts and the big macro accounts, every reply carrying a receipt. You are not arguing, you are annotating. Cheapest attention on X right now and nobody in this category is working it.
5. **Give people a side.** Saturday poll: two tracked voices, opposite calls, same asset, both quotes shown. Recruits both fanbases.

### 2. Revenue — ranked

1. **Do not monetise for 90 days. At all.** Every ask before you have funded the account is a withdrawal, and in a credibility product the first ask also costs you neutrality.
2. **Then: forecaster affiliate links on profile pages — day 91.** Monetises the thing they already want to do.
3. **Then: one sponsor slot, one placement, fixed.** Position four in the weekly thread. One sponsor, not a rotation. Price on the thread's impressions, not follower count.
4. **X revenue share is a byproduct, not a strategy.** It arrives because you are replying 140 times a week. Bank it, don't plan on it.
5. **Kill any subscription conversation until month twelve.**

### 3. The share — ranked

**The weekly pillar thread.** Sunday 18:00 ET — Asia hasn't opened, the macro feed is awake, no news competing. *Names and numbers below are slots filled from graded data — never ship a figure the dataset hasn't resolved.*

> **Tweet 1** (table image attached, no link)
> Tom Lee just got paid.
>
> Feb 11: "S&P 6,800 before year end." It printed Friday — 131 days early. Graded, quoted, timestamped.
>
> Where all 31 macro forecasters stand, week 34 👇

> **2/** Biggest mover: Lyn Alden, +6 places. Two gold calls resolved hit this month. Now 71% on 24 graded calls, average horizon 9 months.

> **3/** Boldness check: the #1 spot is 78% accurate and has never made a bearish call. In a bull market that is a weather report, not a forecast. Boldness-adjusted, they drop to 6th.

> **4/** Somebody flipped. [Name] was long duration in April, quote here. Yesterday, opposite view, quote here. Both logged. We don't grade views — but we do keep them.

> **5/** Consensus this week: 19 of 31 tracked voices are positioned for cuts by March. That's the tightest alignment we've recorded. Historically, that is not a comfortable place to stand.

> **6/** Resolving inside 7 days: four calls, three people. One needs gold above $4,000 by Friday. Receipts go up either way.

> **7/** How this works: falsifiable, time-bound calls only. Price data decides, not us. Rules are public, appeals are open. Full table, every quote, every source → [link]

> **8/** Who's missing? Reply with a macro voice who makes real calls and we'll add them next week.

Link lives in tweet 7 only. Never in tweet 1 — a link in the first tweet is a self-inflicted reach penalty. Ask lives in tweet 8, small and free: three gives, one right hook.

**The forecaster card is the real viral object.** 1080×1350 vertical, one person, name, handle, score, call count, hit rate, and their single most impressive graded call with the quote. Built to be screenshotted by the forecaster. Send it to them by DM before you post it. That is the whole growth strategy in one sentence.

**Ruthless rule on tagging:** tag the top five, never tag a miss. Name misses in text without the @. Dunking buys a spike and spends the asset you are actually building.

### 4. Look, feel, style, format

**Legibility at thumbnail is the only aesthetic requirement that matters.** Test: screenshot the table image, shrink to 25%, view on a phone at arm's length. If you cannot read the top three names and the arrows, it fails.

- **Table image:** 1600×900. **Eight rows maximum** — the brief implies thirty-one and thirty-one is a wall of grey. Name, score, delta arrow. Nothing else.
- **Palette:** near-black ground, off-white type, one green, one red, one accent. Terminal energy, not fintech gradient. It should look like evidence, not marketing.
- **Type:** one sans for names at genuinely large size, one mono for numbers. Mono numbers read as machine-generated, which is the entire brand promise.
- **Fixed furniture:** "MACRO RECEIPTS — WEEK 34" in the same corner every week. Serial identity is why people recognise you mid-scroll.
- **Web:** scoreboard is the homepage, above the fold, no hero, no explainer paragraph. Every row and card gets a one-click "copy image" button. If sharing takes more than one tap you have built a museum.
- **Voice:** flat, factual, zero adjectives about competence. The numbers carry the aggression so the copy doesn't have to.

**Weekly calendar — 26 posts, ~140 replies:**

| Day | Posts |
|---|---|
| Sun 18:00 ET | Pillar thread (8 tweets) = 1 asset |
| Mon | 3 (receipt card, open-call countdown, quote-tweet of a tracked voice with a receipt) |
| Tue | 3 + 1 native video (60s: one call, one chart, one verdict) |
| Wed | 3 + 1 grading-in-public screen recording |
| Thu | 3 + 1 native video |
| Fri | 3 + "Resolving This Week" post |
| Sat | 1 head-to-head poll |
| Daily | 20 replies |

**Content pyramid — one pillar, 32 pieces.** Pillar = weekly scoreboard + briefing. Shredded into: 8 forecaster cards, 5 single-stat cards, 4 quote-cards, 3 native videos, 3 grading screen recordings, 2 chart-with-verdict images, 2 consensus-meter images, 2 open-call countdowns, 1 poll, 1 "who changed their mind" carousel, 1 long-form X post of the briefing in full (native, no link-out).

**Reply protocol, first 90 days.** Tier 1, the tracked forecasters: reply to every relevant post within 60 minutes, always additive, never a gotcha, and DM each one their card before they are ever ranked publicly — no ask, just "this is your page, tell us if a grade is wrong." Tier 2, ~200 macro accounts above 10k followers: 15 replies a day with receipts attached. Tier 3, everyone who replies to you: **reply to all of them, all 90 days** — including the hostile ones, especially the hostile ones, with the rule and the timestamp and no adjectives. Track reply-to-follow ratio weekly. Anything that outperforms by 3x gets rebuilt and reshipped within 48 hours.

### My one non-negotiable
The first public scoreboard post goes up **this Friday** with eight forecasters and ninety backfilled calls, not in twelve weeks with thirty-one. Volume and reps find the format; the dataset can catch up in the background.

### What I would cut
Thirty-one names in v1 — cut to eight, because eight is legible at thumbnail and thirty-one is not. Cut the paywalled-press rows entirely. Cut any post whose only job is announcing that a website exists. Cut every link from tweet 1, forever.

### Where I expect to disagree with the other seats
**Godin** will say cadence beats intensity and 26 posts a week dilutes the promise. Right about the promise, wrong about the number — the platform pays for at-bats, and consistency at volume *is* the cadence. He'll also want the email gate early; I want zero friction for 90 days. **Brunson** will want a lead magnet and a capture link in tweet 1. That link costs more reach than the addresses are worth this quarter. **Musk** will want the roster cut and I agree, but he'll extend the same logic to post volume and cut the shreds as redundant. They are not redundant; they are the search process. **Sutherland** will produce the single best idea in the room and it will require a second read to land. On X there is no second read.

## Seat 3 — The Psychology Seat
### *Rory Sutherland methodology — behavioural science, signalling, reframing*

**Verdict on the brief in one sentence**
The methodology is excellent and the psychology is absent: this brief has built a fair court and forgotten that nobody shares a verdict, they share a feeling — and the feeling on offer here is relief, which the brief never once names.

**The reframe**

The brief thinks it is solving an information problem: *investors can't tell whose views deserve weight*. That is the logical problem. The psychological problem is different and much bigger.

The anxiety is **credulity shame**. The reader has, at some point, listened to a confident man on a podcast, believed him, acted, and lost. He does not talk about this. He now consumes macro commentary in a permanent low-grade crouch, unable to distinguish research from entertainment, and quietly suspecting he is the mark. Every hour he spends listening is an hour that might be being played.

So: people did not want a faster car, they wanted to stop not knowing where the car was. **Investors do not want better forecasts. They want permission to stop listening.** The scoreboard's real product is subtraction — the right to close a tab without guilt. That is what will be felt, and feelings are what travel.

Two consequences the brief hasn't priced in.

First, the emotional posture of the number must be **relief, not superiority**. The brief's instinct ("play the top half," tag the winners) sells a compliment. Compliments are weak distribution; relief is strong. Rewrite the promise from *"here is who is right"* to *"here is how little of this you have to read."*

Second, **what does sharing this signal about the sharer?** On finance X, the highest status is not "I was right" — it is "I am unfoolable." Sharing a scoreboard is an immunity display: *I am the sort of person who checks*. That means the share artefact must make the sharer look sceptical, not partisan. A ranked table asks the sharer to endorse an entire ordering — arguable, tribal, socially expensive. A single graded call asks them only to point at a fact. Design for the second.

### 1. Engagement — ranked

**1. Ship a ritual, not a feed. "Sunday Receipts," 17:00 London / 12:00 ET, every week, never any other time.** The market is shut. Nobody can trade on it. Reading it is therefore reflection, not action — a different, calmer mode, and a mode people build identity around. An appointment becomes a habit; a habit becomes an identity. Refuse to post the table at any other moment even when something big happens; the refusal is the asset.

**2. Make the atomic unit one receipt, not the table.** One call, one named person, one verbatim quote, one date, one outcome, stamped. Weekly the account posts the table; daily-ish it posts single receipts. The receipt is what gets screenshotted into group chats, because it costs the sharer nothing socially.

**3. Counterintuitive proposal #1: tag nobody. Ever.** Every ordinary marketer will tell you to @ the top five. Mechanism: being tagged converts a verdict into a solicitation — the recipient reads it as *someone wants my retweet*, and status-conscious people do not amplify things that flatter them on request. Discovery reads as an indifferent institution having reached a conclusion, which is enormously more shareable and generates zero hostility surface. Ratings agencies do not @ you. **Cheap test:** alternate tagged and untagged weeks for six weeks; measure forecaster-initiated quote-tweets, not impressions. Prediction: untagged weeks win on forecaster amplification and lose slightly on reach — and forecaster amplification is the only reach that compounds.

**4. Counterintuitive proposal #2: grade nothing younger than 90 days, and say so on every asset.** In a medium that pays for speed, sell slowness. Mechanism: slowness in a fast medium is a costly signal — it visibly forgoes the cheap engagement everyone else takes, and cost is what makes a claim believed. It also structurally prevents the product from becoming a hot-take machine, which is the failure mode that kills it. Standing line: *"We do not grade anything until it is old enough to have been embarrassing."* **Cheap test:** run "The Ninety" as an organic monthly post — *what was said ninety days ago today* — for eight weeks before any table exists. If that alone earns followers, the ritual works without the product.

**5. The personal layer is a mute list, not a follow list.** Framing the follow feature as "your forecasters" makes it a fan product. Frame it as **"Your Hundred Minutes"** — if you have 100 minutes of macro listening this month, here is how the record says to spend it, and who to drop. Subtraction is the habit.

### 2. Revenue — ranked

1. **Refuse affiliate revenue from anyone in the top ten, and print that refusal.** The brief's affiliate plan is the single biggest hole in it: you are proposing to be paid a commission by the people you grade. Copy for the site footer: *"We take no commission from anyone in the top ten. If we rank you, we don't sell you."* That forgone money is exactly what makes the ranking believable, and the ranking is the entire business.
2. **One sponsor per quarter, exclusive, with the price published.** A single line of text under the table. Publishing the rate card — *"One sponsor. £6,000 the quarter. One line. No logo."* — is rare enough to be remarkable, and transparency about money is the cheapest trust you will ever buy in finance.
3. **Sell the decision, not the archive — later, and expensively.** When you monetise the reader, sell **"The Weighting"**: a short monthly note stating where the record-weighted consensus differs from the loud consensus. £35/month, no free tier, no trial. People pay to be told what to ignore; they do not pay for more to read.
4. **The Macro-Receipts Annual** — a printed, numbered, limited ledger of the year's graded calls. Sells identity and funds the costly signal simultaneously. A thing on a desk is a permanent advertisement that no algorithm can throttle.

### 3. The share — ranked

**1. Change the name of the score. This is the most important word choice in the product.** Not Score, not Grade, not Accuracy — call it **Weight**. *"Lyn Alden — Weight 74."* Three reasons: it names the reader's actual decision variable (how much attention do I give this person), it is a portfolio word so it lands in-register, and it is not a judgement on a human being, which makes the bottom of the table survivable and the legal posture far safer. Standing definition line: *"Weight is not a verdict on a person. It is how much of your attention their record has earned."* Retire "boldness-adjusted score" from public copy — keep the maths, kill the phrase; it sounds like a video game.

**2. Counterintuitive proposal #3: enter yourself in the table and lose.** "The House" appears as a row, graded by identical rules, and for the first months it will sit mid-table or worse. No ordinary marketer volunteers to rank their own brand below their customers. Mechanism: a referee who is not on the pitch is presumed to have a hidden hand; a referee who is on the pitch and visibly losing has converted an accusation into a shared ordeal, and has pre-emptively destroyed the only strong attack line ("who are you to grade?"). **Cheap test:** two launch-post variants, with and without the House row; measure reply sentiment ratio and forecaster response, not likes.

**3. Publish the appeals you lose, on a page called "Contested."** Every dispute, verbatim, with the outcome. The entries that matter read: *"Grade reversed. We were wrong."* An appeals log showing four reversals out of eleven is worth more than any methodology page, because irreversible self-harm is the only trust signal that cannot be faked.

**4. Print the flaw on the artefact itself.** Not in a footer — on the image: *"What this table cannot see: private positions, paywalled calls, anything said off the record. Some of the best forecasters here will score badly for that reason. We publish it anyway."* Perfection reads as marketing; an admitted limitation reads as truth, and it makes every other claim on the image credible by association.

**5. Make the labour visible.** A permanent counter: *"14,206 minutes of audio. 31 voices. 62 weeks. Every call linked."* And sign every graded call with a human name and date — *"Reviewed by [name], 14 Aug 2026."* A name is a hostage, and hostages are believed.

### 4. Look, feel, style, format

**The aesthetic is an audited document, not a dashboard.** The gravitational pull here is dark-mode neon fintech, which is precisely the visual language of every crypto grift on that timeline; adopting it makes you indistinguishable from the thing you exist to correct. Go the other way and look alien on X — distinctiveness beats differentiation.

Specifics: paper ground `#F4F1EA`, one ink `#1A1A18`, ruled ledger lines, an old-style serif for names (EB Garamond or Source Serif 4), monospace for every figure (IBM Plex Mono). Outcomes as **rubber stamps, slightly off-register** — `HIT` / `MISS` / `VOID` — in one stamp ink only. **No red and green:** red-green is a trading screen and it makes the table feel like a scoreboard of winners, which is the wrong emotion. The whole thing should look printed, filed, and slightly serious to the point of dryness.

Format on X: one 4:5 portrait image, twelve rows maximum, one number per row, the flaw box at the bottom, and an identical footer line every single week — *"Every call linked. Every miss kept. Nothing graded before 90 days."* Repetition of a fixed element builds recognition at thumbnail size. Single receipts go out as 1:1 cards that look like an actual till receipt, torn edge included.

Web: the hero is not a sortable table. It is one receipt, full width, changing daily. Sortable tables are for operators; a single receipt is for humans.

### My one non-negotiable

## Seat 4 — The Reduction Seat
### *Elon Musk method — first principles, the algorithm, critical path*

**Verdict on the brief in one sentence**
This is a well-reasoned 9-month data project wearing the costume of a 3-week distribution product, and the repo name is smarter than the README title — you called it Macro-*Receipts* and then specified a scoreboard, which is the aggregate you cannot build until you have the atom.

**The reframe**
The brief assumes you must first build a corpus, then find calls in it, then eventually resolve some. That's brute-force search: ingest 30 people × all channels × 12 months, hoping falsifiable calls fall out. Reason from physics instead. The irreducible facts: (a) a finite number of people made public, timestamped, falsifiable claims; (b) prices are public; (c) resolution is arithmetic. What you actually need is not a corpus — it is **resolved calls**. Those are a tiny, findable set.

So invert the search. Pick 8–10 macro questions from the last 18 months that already have unambiguous answers. For each, go find who said what beforehand, with the quote and the link. That is search along 8 axes, by hand, instead of ingestion along 30. It costs roughly a tenth as much and produces a strictly better artefact, because every call you surface is already resolved and already famous. §3's "backfill 12 months of everything before building UI" is the most expensive line in the document and it produces zero distribution while it runs.

Idiot index: the raw input of one receipt is a free public quote, a free price, and a date. Marginal cost ≈ $0. The specified pipeline (weekly transcription of 30 people, LLM extraction, human review) puts maybe $30–50 of cost behind each *published* receipt. Index in the hundreds. When the index is that high the design is wrong, not the execution.

### Running the algorithm, in order

**1. Question every requirement.** Defect zero: not one requirement in this README has a name attached. Every item below gets an owner by Monday or it dies.
- *Who said ~30 forecasters?* 30× the ingestion cost for 0× the distribution benefit until a table exists. Cut to **6**.
- *Who said weekly?* Weekly is a newsletter convention. The physics is that calls resolve when the print lands. Event-driven, not calendar.
- *Who said the web app is the destination?* §3 says X-first; §8 specifies four web pages. X's ranking demotes off-platform links. Those two requirements are in direct conflict — the web app loses for v1.
- *Who said affiliate links on forecaster profiles (§3)?* You cannot claim mechanically impartial grading and take a revenue cut from the graded. Delete permanently, not "for v1."
- *Who said appeal process (§5.5)?* If the rule is public and resolution is arithmetic, there is no appeal — there is a bug report. Rename it; it now costs one paragraph instead of a workflow.

**2. Delete.** On the critical path: §4 (principles — this is the product), §5.2 (the X format), §5.4 (grading rules, reduced), §7 (data model, reduced), the narrow slice of §5.6 that resolves prices. Hobbies: §5.1 entirely (following, alerts, notification cadence — retention design for an audience of zero), §5.3 entirely (the Briefing is a second product, a second pipeline, competing with 400 macro newsletters, and it carries none of the moat because it isn't falsifiable), §5.5's appeal machinery, §5.6's cost model, §8's web spec, §9's metric list, §10 (nothing is in scope, so nothing needs excluding).

Deliverables: **#2 is the project.** #3 is one 600-word page, not a workstream. #5 gets replaced by the inverted search above. #1 keeps only the "catchiness" third. #4 and #6 are deleted — a 90-day plan for a thing that ships in 13 days is a document about a document.

Roster: delete WSJ/FT/Bloomberg and Yahoo Finance — those are wire copy, not forecasters. Keep the Fed only as the benchmark line the humans get scored against (the SEP dots are the single best free source of already-resolved, precisely-dated forecasts in existence; grading humans *against the dots* is your sharpest free stat).

Total cut: roughly 80% of roster, ~85% of specified surface area. I'm adding back one thing I deleted — the Fed, repurposed as benchmark — which is about the right 10%.

**3. Simplify.** Two tables, not four. `Forecaster` and `Call`. Delete `Stance` (it exists only to feed the Briefing, which is gone) and fold `SourceItem` into `Call` as url + published_at + quote. Delete `boldness_score`, `partial`, and the hedged-call confidence taxonomy from v1. Verdict is binary: HIT / MISS / VOID. Partial credit is the mechanism by which arguments happen. Rule: if it's hedged, it isn't a call.

**4. Accelerate.** The machine metric is latency from resolution event to published receipt. CPI prints 08:30 ET; the receipts are live by 09:15. Nobody in this space operates at that clock speed, and speed is the thing that cannot be copied by a smarter analyst with a slower process.

**5. Automate — last.** Automate now: price lookup and resolution (zero judgement), the deadline/threshold watcher cron that triggers the publishing loop, and the receipt-card render (HTML → headless screenshot → PNG, ~30 lines). Do **not** automate extraction. At 6 people and ~50 calls, a human reading and pasting is faster than building the extractor and it's how you learn the schema. Automating extraction before you understand what a call looks like bakes the error into the moat. Revisit at roster 20+. Do not automate posting — a human presses send, forever.

### 1. Engagement — ranked
1. **Ship the atom, not the aggregate.** One receipt: the quote, the date said, the price then, the price now, the verdict. That is the whole engagement engine.

### 4. Look, feel, style, format

Fixed layout, never redesigned — the constraint is recognisability at 400px while someone scrolls at speed, not beauty. It should read as an audit document, not a media brand; aesthetic credibility here comes from looking mechanical. Web: **one page** — a reverse-chronological list of receipts plus the rules. Ignore: followers, impressions, read-completion, and especially click-through — optimising CTR pushes you to post links, which X demotes.

## Seat 5 — The Funnel Seat
### *Russell Brunson methodology — funnels, offers, value ladders, list ownership*

**Verdict on the brief in one sentence**
This is the best structural setup for a funnel I have seen in a long time — a free hook with built-in virality, a proprietary dataset, and a Dream 100 that is contractually obliged to react to you — and the brief then throws it away by ruling out the only revenue line it actually owns.

**The reframe**
The brief confuses **the hook with the lead magnet.** The scoreboard is not the lead magnet — it is bait that lives on rented land. Nothing in §5 converts earned traffic into owned traffic. There is no email capture anywhere in a 156-line document, and the three named revenue lines — sponsorship, affiliate, X rev-share — are all *someone else's* money: an advertiser's, a forecaster's, a landlord's. Sponsorship needs scale you won't have for a year. X rev-share is eviction risk with a stipend. And **affiliate links on forecaster profiles are a slow bullet in the moat**: the moment you earn a commission on a forecaster's service, every ranking you publish is purchasable in principle, and your §4.6 "same rigour for everyone" defence is gone for what will realistically be $300 a month. Cut them.

On "do not plan on subscriptions early": the ruling conflates *subscription* with *paywall*. Never paywall the scoreboard — that's the acquisition engine and it stays free forever. But the Briefing is explicitly labelled "retention," and retention is the thing you charge for. You also have something almost nobody has at launch: a 12-month backfill. **You have a sellable product before you have an audience.** Waiting 12 months to charge means arriving at month 12 with a large list and zero purchase data — you will know who claps and nothing about who pays. Charge in week six. Small.

### 1. Engagement — ranked

1. **Ship the shareable unit as a "Receipt," literally.** A portrait image styled as a printed till receipt: forecaster name at top, line items (`GOLD >$4000 BY 12/31 ....... HIT`), `SUBTOTAL: 14 CALLS`, `TOTAL: 61% / BOLDNESS 1.4x`, barcode, and the URL printed at the bottom where a receipt prints the store address.
