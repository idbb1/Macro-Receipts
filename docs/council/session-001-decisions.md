# Council Session 001 — Synthesis and Decisions

**Date:** 28 August 2026
**Input:** five independent contributions, [`session-001-brainstorm.md`](./session-001-brainstorm.md)
**Status:** recommendations. Items marked **[NEEDS A CALL]** are the owner's decision, not the council's.

---

## The headline

The council did not argue about tactics. It converged, unprompted and from five different
directions, on one structural claim:

> **The product is the receipt. The scoreboard is a derivative of it.**

Five out of five seats reached this independently, having never seen each other's answers.
The brief has it the other way round — it specifies a scoreboard and treats individual graded
calls as rows in it. Every seat argued the aggregate cannot be built until the atom exists, and
that the atom is also the thing that travels.

The test that settles it is the Positioning seat's: strip the branding off a ranked table of
names and numbers and it is meaningless without the method attached. Strip the branding off a
single receipt — quote, date, source, outcome — and it is still completely legible and still
devastating.

**Consequence:** the first public artefact is one receipt, not a table. The table arrives at
roughly receipt #50.

## The five real conflicts, and how they resolve

### 1. Tagging — Distribution vs Psychology

Distribution wants the top five tagged and their cards DM'd to them first. Psychology wants
**nobody tagged, ever**, on the reasoning that a tag converts a verdict into a solicitation:
status-conscious people do not amplify things that flatter them on request, whereas an
indifferent institution reaching a conclusion is far more shareable and creates no hostility
surface. Ratings agencies do not @ you.

**Resolution — take the union, not the average.** DM the forecaster their card before publication
(Distribution's move, which is where the value actually is) and tag nobody in the public post
(Psychology's). Private compliment, public indifference. This was not either seat's proposal; it
is what survives both arguments.

Psychology also supplied the tiebreak experiment, which we should run regardless: alternate
tagged and untagged weeks for six weeks and measure **forecaster-initiated quote-tweets**, not
impressions. Forecaster amplification is the only reach that compounds.

### 2. Volume — Distribution (26 posts/week) vs Psychology (a weekly ritual, scarcity)

**Resolution — the Positioning seat's distinction breaks the tie:** volume of *receipts*, yes;
volume of *takes*, no. The account's standing rests on having no view, so every opinion it
publishes devalues every receipt it publishes.

Standing rule: **the account posts only receipts, open-call clocks, and the weekly table.**
Nothing else. Under that constraint Distribution gets unbounded volume as the dataset grows,
and Psychology keeps the institutional scarcity, because an institution posting forty facts a
week still reads as an institution. A brand posting forty opinions does not.

### 3. Speed vs the 90-day cooling rule — Reduction vs Psychology

Reduction wants receipts live within 45 minutes of the print that resolved them. Psychology wants
nothing graded until it is 90 days old, as a costly signal: visibly forgoing cheap engagement is
what makes the claim believed.

**These only look incompatible.** They govern different clocks. Psychology's 90 days is the age
of the *call* — the gap between statement and deadline. Reduction's 45 minutes is latency between
*resolution* and *publication*. A call made in March with a December deadline is 295 days old and
can still be published within the hour of resolving.

**Resolution — adopt both, stated as one rule:** *a call is only gradeable if at least 90 days
separate the statement from its deadline; once it resolves, it publishes the same day.* The only
thing this excludes is the short-horizon hot take, which is the failure mode both seats were
trying to prevent anyway.

### 4. Email capture — Funnel (day one) vs Reduction/Distribution (later) vs Psychology (never a gated PDF)

**Resolution — the Positioning seat's version is the only one that satisfies all four.** Not a
lead magnet. A per-forecaster notification subscription, on every profile page:

> *"Follow [name]'s calls. We'll email you twice: when they make a new dated call, and when one
> resolves. Nothing else."*

Anticipated, personal and relevant by construction. It ships day one (Funnel's timing), it is not
a PDF-for-email gate (Psychology satisfied), and it lives on the site rather than in the X post,
so it adds no friction to distribution (Distribution satisfied). **The gated "Receipts File" PDF
is not adopted.**

### 5. Revenue — the Funnel's six-rung ladder vs three seats wanting almost none of it

**Resolution — the decisive vote is that the Funnel seat itself killed the brief's affiliate
plan.** When the revenue seat and the reduction seat independently agree that a revenue line
destroys the asset, that line is dead. Four of five seats cut it explicitly.

The ranked revenue plan below takes the Funnel seat's strongest idea (sell the dataset, not the
audience — which the Reduction seat proposed independently at a similar price) and drops the
consumer funnel machinery, because visible funnel apparatus bolted to an audit destroys the
audit.

## 1. Engagement — the decided plan

1. **Ship the atom.** One receipt per resolved call, permalinked at `/r/2025-03-11-gold-4000`,
   with its own image. This is the engagement engine and the SEO surface at once.
2. **The open-call clock, permanently on-site and in every weekly post.** *"On the clock: 11 calls
   resolve before 31 October."* Resolved calls are history and get one clap; an open call is a
   story the reader wants finished.
3. **Event-triggered publication.** The receipt goes out the day the print resolves it, not on a
   content calendar. Nobody in this category operates at that clock speed.
4. **One fixed weekly ritual** — same slot, no exceptions, 100 weeks running. The reliability *is*
   the product demo: we are the people who keep records.
5. **Grading in public.** A short screen recording of a call being resolved against the published
   rule. This turns the methodology page, which nobody reads, into the most trust-building
   content available.
6. **"Send us a receipt."** Readers submit a claim they remember; you source it, timestamp it,
   grade it, credit them by handle. Cheapest possible sourcing, and it converts an audience into
   contributors.

**Deferred to ~200 receipts:** streaks, rank moves, flip alerts, reader predictions. All are good
and all require a dataset that does not exist yet.

## 2. Revenue — the decided plan

Ranked by how little each one touches the ranking's credibility.

1. **Dataset and media licensing — ~$25k/yr institutional, ~$5k/yr small.** Proposed independently
   by both the Funnel and Reduction seats. The buyer is neither someone you score nor someone
   whose trust you need, so it carries no conflict surface at all. A media outlet paying to cite
   "per Macro-Receipts" makes you the referee rather than a competitor.
2. **One sponsor, restricted, with the restriction published.** *No sponsor may be a person or firm
   we score, sell forecasts, or sell a view.* Tools and infrastructure only. The restriction is
   worth more as public copy than the marginal dollar it costs. Consider publishing the rate card
   itself — transparency about money is the cheapest trust available in finance.
3. **Weighted consensus, later, behind a wall.** Consensus weighted by track record is the one
   number that cannot be reproduced without this dataset. Keep raw consensus free forever; that
   single line is the only honest paywall here.
4. **X revenue share.** Bank it; never design for it. The moment a payout influences what you post
   you have become the thing you are auditing.

**Cut permanently:** affiliate links to graded forecasters, paid inclusion, paid removal, verified-
track-record badges sold to the scored, tripwires, order bumps, exit-intent popups.

**The line that pays for all of it,** in the footer of every page: **"We take no money from anyone
we score."** It is also what lets a top-ranked forecaster share their card without being accused
of having bought the position.

## 3. The share — the decided plan

1. **Rename the score to Weight.** Unopposed, and the single best word in the session. *"Lyn Alden —
   Weight 74."* It names the reader's actual decision variable (how much attention has this record
   earned), it lands in-register as a portfolio word, and it is not a verdict on a human being —
   which makes the bottom of the table survivable and the legal posture materially safer.
   Standing definition: *"Weight is not a verdict on a person. It is how much of your attention
   their record has earned."*
2. **Add a second, non-ranked number: On Record** — the count of dated, falsifiable, still-public
   calls. This is the Positioning seat's deepest reframe: accuracy is partly luck and manufactures
   one friend and twenty-nine enemies, whereas accountability is entirely a choice and is
   something a forecaster is *proud* to display at a 48% hit rate. It converts the roster from
   adversaries into participants, and it moves the enemy from *wrong forecasters* — named humans —
   to *claims made without a date*, which is a behaviour and cannot sue you.
3. **Publish the top-ranked person's misses**, under their headline number, at equal typographic
   weight. It makes them more credible when they share it, removes any charge of flattery, and
   means the bottom of the table is never needed for drama.
4. **Put the House on the table.** Run our own graded row under identical rules, and expect to lose
   for months. A referee off the pitch is presumed to have a hidden hand; a referee on the pitch
   and visibly losing has destroyed the only strong attack line — *who are you to grade?*
5. **Publish the appeals we lose,** on a page called *Contested*, verbatim, including the entries
   that read "Grade reversed. We were wrong." Irreversible self-harm is the only trust signal
   that cannot be faked.
6. **Print the flaw on the artefact**, not in a footer: *"What this table cannot see: private
   positions, paywalled calls, anything said off the record. Some of the best forecasters here
   will score badly for that reason. We publish it anyway."* An admitted limitation makes every
   other claim on the image credible by association.
7. **Give away the dataset.** Weekly CSV, CC-BY. It makes every quant on X an unpaid auditor, and
   the first person to find an error and receive a public correction becomes the loudest advocate.

**The promise sentence, used unchanged everywhere:**
**"We write down what they said. Then we write down what happened."**

**Never ships:** "worst forecaster," "exposed," "destroyed," "aged like milk," bottom-five lists,
ranks counted from the bottom, or any mocking quote-tweet. Everyone's full record is public at
identical rigour — but ranks are *numbered* only in the top tier; below it, alphabetical. That
satisfies §4.6 without building a ladder to the bottom.

## 4. Look, feel, style, format — the decided spec

Four of five seats independently specified almost the same thing: **an off-white paper ground,
near-black ink, monospace numerals, and exactly one accent colour reserved for the verdict stamp.**
The Distribution seat dissented toward a near-black terminal aesthetic, but its actual requirement
is thumbnail legibility, which paper satisfies at least as well.

The reasoning is convergent and worth keeping: every macro product on X looks like a dark-mode
Bloomberg terminal, which is also the visual language of every grift on that timeline. Adopting it
makes you indistinguishable from the thing you exist to correct. Looking like a filed document is
both more distinctive and more on-message.

```
GROUND    #F5F2EC   paper
INK       #14140F   near-black, all text and rules
STAMP     #B3261E   verdict stamp ONLY — appears nowhere else in the system
MUTED     #6B675E   metadata, source lines, timestamps
```

- **No red/green pairing.** It reads as a trading screen and makes the table feel like a
  leaderboard of winners, which is the wrong emotion. One stamp ink, used once per card.
- **Two typefaces, one job each.** The quote is the hero, set large in a text serif (Source Serif
  4 / EB Garamond). Every figure — dates, tickers, thresholds, prices — in mono (IBM Plex Mono).
  Mono numerals read as machine-generated, which is the entire brand promise.
- **Rules and hairlines. No cards, shadows, gradients, glass, or charts on share images.**
- **Verdict as a rubber stamp**, boxed, slightly off-register: `HIT` / `MISS` / `VOID`.
- **Fixed furniture, never redesigned.** A redesign resets recognition to zero; sameness is the
  compounding asset.
- **URL burned into every image**, bottom-centre, where a real receipt prints the store address.
  The screenshot is the advertisement.

**Acceptance gate (Distribution seat's test, adopted as a hard rule):** shrink the image to 25%
and view it on a phone at arm's length. If the name and the verdict are not readable, it does not
ship. This caps the quote at ~18 words and the weekly table at **12 rows maximum**.

**Web:** the homepage is one receipt, full-bleed — the week's most interesting resolution — with
"See the full record" below the fold. A sortable table as a homepage teaches the visitor this is a
database; a receipt teaches them it is a case. One-tap "copy image" on every row and card.

### The receipt card

```
RECEIPT #0412                        MACRO-RECEIPTS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────
[Name] · [outlet] · 11 March 2025

"[exact verbatim quote, ≤18 words]"

CALL      Gold ≥ $4,000/oz
DEADLINE  31 Dec 2025
RESULT    [ HIT ]   $4,118 · LBMA PM · 31 Dec 2025
ON RECORD 295 days
───────────────────────────────────────────────────
Reviewed by [name], [date]
source: [url]                            rules: /rules
```

**Hard production rule, from the Positioning seat, and the most important line in this document:**
**never mock this card up with an invented quote — not even internally, not even in a throwaway
draft.** Fabricated screenshots leak, and one fabricated quote attributed to a real person ends
this project permanently. Use `[QUOTE PENDING]` in every draft, wireframe and test fixture.

### The weekly post

Merged from the Distribution seat's thread structure and the Psychology seat's constraints.

- One 4:5 image, **12 rows max**, one number per row, flaw box at the bottom.
- **Identical footer every week:** *"Every call linked. Every miss kept. Nothing graded before 90 days."*
- Post 1 is the biggest resolution of the week in plain language + the image. **No link. No tags.**
- Then: biggest mover · the boldness caveat on the leader · who flipped (both quotes, no
  commentary) · consensus · what resolves in the next 7 days.
- Link and methodology in the **first self-reply**.
- Closing ask, small and free: *"Who's missing? Reply with a macro voice who makes real calls."*

## Ship sequence

The Reduction seat's inverted search is adopted, and it is the cheapest idea in the session:
**do not ingest 30 people × 12 months hoping calls fall out.** Pick 8–10 macro questions from the
last 18 months that already have unambiguous answers, then go find who said what beforehand. That
is search along 8 axes by hand instead of ingestion along 30, at roughly a tenth of the cost, and
every call it surfaces is already resolved and already famous.

| When | Ship |
|---|---|
| **10 Sep 2026** | First public receipt. 6–8 forecasters, ~12 hand-sourced resolved calls. No table, no site beyond a permalink. |
| **Weeks 2–5** | Receipts only, event-triggered. Rules page live. Per-forecaster email follow live. Contested page live *before* the first dispute. |
| **8 Oct 2026** | First weekly table, 12 rows, at ~50 receipts. The House row included. |
| **From ~200 receipts** | Streaks, rank moves, flip alerts, weighted consensus. |

**The one metric:** published, sourced, resolved receipts per week. Target 10/week by 8 October.
**The one guardrail:** disputes *upheld* — target zero. (§9 counts disputes; the count is
meaningless, upheld is everything.)
**Ignore:** followers, impressions, read-completion, and especially click-through — optimising CTR
pushes you toward posting links, which the platform demotes.

   **[NEEDS A CALL]**
